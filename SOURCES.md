# Prayer texts and sources

Reviewed November 2, 2026. Current Catholic is the default; Contemporary English is the other selectable option. Every prayer opens to its complete text. Source information is available in Settings. The reading view suppresses source/wording controls; concise required copyright credits appear before the reading controls for applicable licensed prayers, with detailed acknowledgment in the separate recording view.

## Current Catholic

The app displays the customary Catholic prayers with wording checked against these primary sources:

- [USCCB: Prayers of the Rosary](https://www.usccb.org/prayers/prayers-rosary): Apostles’ Creed, Hail Mary, Hail Holy Queen.
- [USCCB: Our Father](https://www.usccb.org/prayers/our-father): the Catholic Our Father, without the Protestant doxology.
- [USCCB: Glory Be](https://www.usccb.org/prayers/glory-be-doxology): the customary doxology, using “Holy Spirit.”
- [USCCB: How to Pray the Rosary](https://www.usccb.org/how-to-pray-the-rosary): Fatima Prayer, closing dialogue and prayer, mystery names and sequence. The opening Sign of the Cross uses its customary Catholic formula with “Holy Spirit.”

These are prayer texts, not copied website explanations, Scripture readings or meditations. The customary devotional forms have historical antecedents; this app does not claim that USCCB grants a blanket license for its website or all modern liturgical translations.

## Contemporary English

- Our Father: [ELLC Lord’s Prayer](https://www.englishtexts.org/the-lords-prayer), complete, including its doxology. This is the ELLC wording, which differs from the Church of England’s contemporary text at the trial/temptation petition.
- Apostles’ Creed: [ELLC text](https://www.englishtexts.org/the-apostles-creed).
- Glory Be: [ELLC Gloria Patri, layout 1](https://www.englishtexts.org/gloria-patri).
- [ELLC permissions](https://www.englishtexts.org/permissions) allow free use without prior permission with attribution: English translations of the Lord’s Prayer, Apostles’ Creed and Gloria Patri, © 1998 English Language Liturgical Consultation (ELLC), and used by permission. www.englishtexts.org. The notice year follows the holder’s current requested acknowledgment.
- Sign of the Cross, Hail Mary, Fatima Prayer, Hail Holy Queen, and closing prayer: original Share My Prayer adaptations of the customary prayers, dedicated to the public domain under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). They are not officially approved Catholic translations; this distinction is stated in the source details.
- [Church of England Lord’s Prayer](https://www.churchofengland.org/faith-life/what-we-believe/lords-prayer) remains a comparison link. Its [copyright conditions](https://www.churchofengland.org/copyright) include app/software restrictions; its wording is not substituted for the directly permitted ELLC text.

## Mysteries

All 20 announcement prompts are original ordinal announcement sentences using the customary mystery names, checked against the USCCB rosary guide. No USCCB meditations or biblical quotations are copied.

## Suppressed legacy version

Traditional Catholic is hidden from Settings and is never selected by default. An existing Traditional preference is interpreted as Current Catholic; an explicit Contemporary English preference is retained. Legacy recordings retain their original version keys and stay in local storage and backups. They are not silently relabeled as Current Catholic recordings.

Historical text data is retained only for legacy compatibility: [Baltimore Catechism No. 1](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/14551), [Baltimore Catechism No. 3](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/14553), and [How to Say the Rosary (1937)](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/How_to_Say_the_Rosary). These historical sources are public domain in the United States; status elsewhere may differ. [All Saints/FSSP prayers](https://www.fsspminneapolis.org/prayers) was an initial comparison source, not a grant of app rights.
